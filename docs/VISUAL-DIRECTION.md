# Visual Direction

## North star
The public site should feel closer to a premium automotive product story, editorial photo essay and modern brand film than to a local-service landing page.

Reference principle: learn from the pacing, confidence and product focus of strong automotive brand experiences such as Scout Motors, but do not clone layouts, transitions, typography or assets.

## What creates the premium feeling
Not effects. Not expensive-car imagery. Use:
- exceptional real photography/video
- deliberate crops and close material detail
- confident typography
- controlled negative space
- contrast between quiet and dramatic moments
- scroll pacing
- real proof
- meaningful motion

## Visual narrative
The same hero vehicle should appear across time and restoration stages whenever feasible. Continuity matters more than quantity of cars.

The public experience may move between:
- near-black cinematic scenes for headlights/light
- warm off-white editorial scenes for interior/material
- neutral workshop/process scenes

This tonal movement should support the story, not become random section theming.

## Typography
Use a distinctive display face paired with a highly readable grotesk/sans. A restrained mono face may be used for factual metadata (vehicle, year, duration, case number), not as a fake diagnostic aesthetic.

Rules:
- large type is compositional, not just a heading size
- avoid repeated identical section heading patterns
- line breaks are art-directed
- Serbian Latin copy must render correctly
- never sacrifice mobile legibility for poster aesthetics

## Geometry
Prefer clean planes, image edges, typographic alignment and occasional intentional overlap. Minimal rounding. Cards are used only where the information model truly benefits from containment.

## Photography
Real work is the strongest asset.

Shot categories to plan for:
- vehicle establishing shots
- identical before/after angles
- extreme headlight oxidation detail
- polishing process detail
- headlight beam/result at dusk/night
- seat/fabric macro
- extractor/liquid proof
- interior wide before/after
- hands/tools/process
- final reveal

Do not use stock imagery once launch-quality real media exists.

## Motion language
Every motion must express one of:
- time
- reveal
- cleaning/restoration
- light
- material
- progression through the story

Examples:
- restrained masked image reveal
- same-angle state transition
- headlight illumination
- text entering only after visual evidence is understood
- subtle image depth on desktop

Avoid:
- constant parallax
- scroll hijacking
- cursor gimmicks needed to understand content
- gratuitous 3D
- animations that delay booking

## Before/after
Do not reduce the entire experience to generic sliders. Use different truthful reveal mechanisms: same-angle cut, hold-to-reveal on touch, editorial sequence, controlled slider where comparison precision matters.

## Mobile
Mobile is a designed version, not collapsed desktop.

On mobile:
- one visual idea per viewport
- shorter copy
- preserve cinematic imagery with intentional crops
- use touch-native comparison
- avoid horizontal gestures that fight browser navigation unless strongly justified
- booking CTA must remain easy to reach

## Anti-template test
Before approving a screen, ask:
1. Could a dentist, gym or SaaS company use this layout by swapping images/text?
2. Is the design relying on cards, pills and rounded containers rather than composition?
3. Is the automotive identity coming from actual material/light/vehicle storytelling, or only from a car photo?
4. Is there a memorable visual idea that belongs to this specific story?
5. Does it remain credible as a real business?

If answers expose generic structure, redesign before polishing.
