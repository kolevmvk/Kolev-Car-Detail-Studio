# Sponsors & Technical Partners

## Principle
Sponsorship must increase perceived craftsmanship and credibility. It must never make the site feel ad-supported.

## Never use
- generic banner ads
- intrusive sponsor popups
- rotating ad carousels
- unrelated placements
- cheap logo walls
- fake endorsements
- paid claims disguised as independent proof

## Valid sponsor contexts
Sponsors may appear through:
- tools actually used
- products actually used
- technical partner credits
- sponsored but transparently labeled transformations
- case studies
- branded educational/process content
- social distribution
- booking confirmation only when contextually relevant

## Public presentation
Preferred language:
- `Technical partner`
- `Products used`
- `Process supported by`
- `Presented with`

Attribution should be visually restrained and integrated into the process/case composition.

## Sponsor data model
Plan for:
- sponsor
- category
- logo/assets
- website/social URLs
- campaign start/end
- allowed placements
- disclosure text
- associated services
- associated cases/jobs
- products/tools
- impressions
- clicks
- social appearances

## Sponsored case
A full transformation may be sponsored, but the commercial relationship must be clear. The sponsor cannot dictate false performance claims.

Example structure:
`CASE 042 — Headlight restoration`
`Technical partner: Brand X`
`Products used: ...`

## Analytics
The owner should eventually be able to report sponsor value with factual data:
- website impressions
- case appearances
- outbound clicks
- content appearances
- referred booking events where attributable

Do not promise analytics granularity the implementation cannot reliably measure.

## Editorial rule
The car and craftsmanship remain primary. Sponsor presence is secondary and contextual.

If removing the sponsor logo breaks the composition, the placement is too dominant.
